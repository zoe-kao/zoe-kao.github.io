COMPUTATIONAL FIRE DYNAMICS

Microgravity Flame Spread Between Parallel Samples

A computational investigation of how separation distance and imposed airflow influence thermal interaction, oxygen availability, and flame behavior between closely spaced solid fuels in microgravity.

CFD Fortran SIMPLER Tecplot MATLAB Combustion

01 — OVERVIEW

The Research Problem

In microgravity, buoyancy-driven flow is greatly reduced, making imposed airflow and geometry especially important to flame spread.

This project investigates flame interaction between two thin, parallel solid-fuel samples. One sample is ignited, and the resulting flame can transfer heat to the neighboring sample while simultaneously consuming oxygen in the gap.

My work examines the competing effects of increased thermal coupling and reduced oxygen availability as sample separation distance and imposed airflow are varied.

02 — RESEARCH QUESTION

How do geometry and airflow change flame interaction?

The central goal is to understand how separation distance between fuel samples and imposed flow velocity affect the local thermal and chemical environment surrounding the flame.

03 — COMPUTATIONAL MODEL

Simulating coupled flame-spread physics

Simulations are performed using an in-house Fortran CFD code that solves the coupled flow, pressure, heat-transfer, species-transport, combustion, radiation, and solid-fuel pyrolysis problem.

The flow field is solved using the SIMPLER pressure-velocity coupling algorithm. Simulation output is processed and visualized using Tecplot and MATLAB.

05 — ANALYSIS

Competing thermal and oxygen effects

Reducing the gap between samples can increase thermal interaction and secondary-surface heating. At the same time, stronger confinement can increase oxygen depletion near the flame.

Because these mechanisms compete with one another, the effect of separation distance is not necessarily monotonic. I analyze temperature, oxygen, velocity, reaction rate, and surface heating together rather than relying on a single flame metric.

06 — DATA EXTRACTION

Looking beyond contour plots

Quantitative data are extracted along physically meaningful locations within the domain, including the center of the gap and the surface of the secondary fuel sample.

These profiles allow comparison of streamwise velocity, gas temperature, oxygen concentration, secondary-surface temperature, and reaction rate between simulations.

07 — TAKEAWAY

Geometry changes more than just heat transfer.

Closely spaced fuels interact through a combination of thermal feedback, oxygen transport, chemical reaction, and flow modification. Understanding all of these effects together is necessary to explain changes in flame behavior and secondary ignition.
